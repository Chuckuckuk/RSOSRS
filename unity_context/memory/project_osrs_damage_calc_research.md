Researched the real oldschool.runescape.wiki mechanics behind damage types and calculations, triggered by reviewing the Stage 8 composite boss (Nyxandra) design doc for accuracy. See [[project-boss-mechanics-taxonomy]] and [[project-scope-moment-to-moment-only]] for related context.

**Key facts:**
- **Combat triangle** (Melee > Ranged > Magic > Melee) is expressed through *defensive armor bonuses* per style, not a hard damage block — any style can effectively kill any other style; the triangle only affects how tanky a given armor choice is against each incoming style.
- **Three real accuracy/damage styles only: Melee, Ranged, Magic.** Each attack rolls accuracy (attack roll vs. defence roll) and has its own max-hit formula. Melee max hit = `floor((effective Strength × (Strength bonus + 64) + 320) / 640)`. Accuracy: attack roll = `effective Attack × (Attack bonus + 64)`; monster defence roll = `(Defence level + 9) × (style Defence bonus + 64)`; hit chance uses one of two formulas depending on which roll is larger. This is real but substantially more complex than this project's current `random.Next(min,max)` `DamageResolver` — deliberately simplified for now (see `OSRS_COMBAT_PLAN.md` open question #3, "damage model fidelity"); revisit with these actual formulas in hand if full fidelity is ever warranted.
- **"Typeless" is NOT a 4th parallel damage style.** It's old terminology (pre-2017 Chambers of Xeric) for an attack with **100% prayer penetration** — it still rolls accuracy against the normal defensive stat of whichever real style it is (Melee/Ranged/Magic); it just ignores protection prayers' blocking regardless of which one is active. Modern convention treats this as a continuous 0–100% "prayer penetration" value, not a binary flag. Real wiki phrasing confirms it's a *modifier*, not a replacement style — e.g. Sol Heredit's page: "44 typeless **melee** damage," not just "44 typeless damage."
- **"Pierce" and "ignore" are two different points on that same 0–100% spectrum, not synonyms** (user correction, confirmed against real bosses): "piercing" = partial penetration — the correct protection prayer still reduces the hit, just not to zero (e.g. **Phantom Muspah's ranged attacks** pierce: Protect from Missiles helps but doesn't fully block). "Ignoring" = 100% penetration — the correct prayer does nothing at all (e.g. **Doom of Mokhaiotl's ground slams** ignore prayer outright). A single boolean flag can't express this; it needs a continuous value (0.0 = normal full block, something in between = pierce, 1.0 = ignore).
- **Undead attribute convention:** the real OSRS counter for Undead monsters is the **Salve Amulet** (+16.67%/+20% Attack+Strength, or also Ranged/Magic for imbued/enchanted-imbued versions) and the **Crumble Undead** spell (Magic-only, undead-exclusive, max 15 dmg) — not an automatic fire weakness. Fire weakness on undead (zombies, Undead Druids, Vorkath all have it) is a separate, per-monster elemental weakness added in a 2025 update, not an automatic consequence of the Undead attribute itself.
- Protection prayers block ~100% of NPC damage by default; in PvP they only reduce damage by 40% (not currently relevant to this project's solo/PvE scope, noted in case party/PvP is ever revisited).
- **Protection prayer is checked at the exact tick the attack's accuracy/damage roll happens, never averaged or sampled across the telegraph window.** This is the whole basis of real "prayer flicking"/"tick-eating" — a player can hold the WRONG prayer for an attack's entire telegraph and take zero damage by switching to the correct one exactly on the resolve tick, and symmetrically can hold the CORRECT prayer the whole telegraph and still eat full damage by dropping it one tick early. Confirmed via the wiki's Prayer/Tick-eating pages and TzTok-Jad's page (its magic attack specifically "has no delay if the player is directly adjacent... and therefore cannot be tick-eaten in this situation" — the general case elsewhere does have a calculation-vs-landing delay for ranged/magic that melee doesn't, but that delay is about when a MISS-cap/heal can be snuck in, not about which tick's prayer state matters for the roll itself).
- **Three distinct NPC attack-timing archetypes** (user's own categorization, 2026-10-04, refining the single fact above into a taxonomy this project's engine now models explicitly):
  1. **Traditional** (the majority of NPCs) — the roll happens the instant the attack is registered/the animation begins, with no reliable multi-tick telegraph to watch for. Prayer must already be active when the animation starts; reacting after it begins is too late.
  2. **Jad-style** (TzTok-Jad) — a multi-tick telegraph animation, then the roll happens at the END of it — behaving exactly like Traditional from that point on. The only shape this project's `NpcCombatState` supported before this categorization.
  3. **Doom-style** (Doom of Mokhaiotl) — little/no telegraph, but the projectile/effect itself is visibly distinguishable by attack style, and the roll is DEFERRED until it actually lands, giving a genuinely full late-reaction window during the travel itself (not a telegraph at all).
  All three reduce to two numbers on an attack: how long the telegraph is, and how long the post-telegraph travel/impact delay is (zero for the first two, nonzero for the third) — see `AttackDefinition.TelegraphTicks`/`ImpactDelayTicks` below.
- **"When prayer is checked" and "when HP actually changes" are TWO SEPARATE ticks for ranged/magic, even for Traditional and Jad-style** (user-requested follow-up, 2026-10-04, researched via the **Phoenix necklace**'s wiki page). The roll (and prayer check) happens at registration/telegraph-end as already established above, but the resulting FIXED damage amount then sits in a real, named, inspectable OSRS mechanic — the **"attack buffer"/"damage queue"** — for the projectile's travel time, before it actually reduces HP. Melee has no travel time, so for melee the two ticks are the same and this was never visible before. Confirmed this is a real queue, not just an abstract delay, because: **the Phoenix necklace's auto-heal (30% max HP at ≤20% max HP) doesn't just cap the ONE hit that triggered it — it CLEARS THE DEFENDER'S ENTIRE BUFFER.** This is exactly why an early sub-hit of a multi-hit special attack (dragon claws' 4 hits, dark bow's 2) triggering the necklace cancels the damage of the LATER sub-hits too, even though those were already rolled and queued before the heal happened — you see the attack animation and projectile visually land, but take no damage, because the entry was removed from the buffer before its scheduled tick.

**Why it mattered / how it was applied:** Stage 1's `AttackStyle` enum had `Typeless` as a co-equal 4th value alongside Melee/Ranged/Magic, conflating "what style this attack is" with "does it bypass prayer." Fixed by removing `Typeless` from `AttackStyle` (down to Melee/Ranged/Magic only) and pulling a `PrayerPenetration` float (0.0–1.0, not a bool) forward onto `AttackDefinition` now, while the enum was still cheap to change — nothing in the codebase referenced `AttackStyle.Typeless` yet. First pass wrongly modelled this as a bool (`PiercesProtection`) before the pierce-vs-ignore distinction above was raised; corrected to a float so Stage 2's `ReactionRequirement`/`PlayerReactionState` work can express the whole spectrum, not just the two extremes. Also fixed: `STAGE8_COMPOSITE_BOSS.md` (Nyxandra) was missing the Salve Amulet/Crumble Undead angle despite having the Undead attribute, used bare "typeless" phrasing instead of the real "typeless + style" pattern, and had both its prayer-bypass mechanics (Keening Wail, The Deep Toll) modelled as full-ignore when the Stage 8 requirement specifically asks for "prayer-**piercing**" — Deep Toll was changed to a genuine partial pierce to match.

**How to apply:** when writing any future damage-type-facing content (data, docs, wiki-style pages) or building Stage 2's `ReactionRequirement`/`PlayerReactionState`, use real terminology precisely — three styles only, prayer-penetration as a float property of an attack (not a style, not a bool), "pierce" and "ignore" are different points on that spectrum and should be named/valued accordingly (don't default every prayer-bypass mechanic to 100%), "typeless magic damage" phrasing (not bare "typeless"), and remember Undead-tagged content should mention Salve Amulet/Crumble Undead alongside any elemental weakness. **Verified correct (2026-10-04) against this project's actual code:** `ReactionResolver.GetDamageMultiplier` is only ever called from `NpcCombatant.RunNpcAi`/`BossCombatant.RunBossAi` in the branch where `NpcCombatState.Advance` just returned `true` (the resolve tick) — reaction state during the telegraph's earlier ticks is never read at all, matching the precise-tick mechanic above. `PrayerCheckTimingTests.cs` locks this in end-to-end (advances a real `NpcCombatState` through a multi-tick telegraph with the wrong prayer, switches correct only on the final tick, asserts full mitigation, and the mirror case). If `NpcCombatState`/`BossCombatant`/`NpcCombatant` are ever refactored to snapshot `PlayerReactionState` earlier (e.g. at telegraph-start, for some future caching reason), that test will catch the regression — don't "optimize" that read without rerunning it. **Implemented the three-archetype taxonomy (2026-10-04):** `AttackDefinition` gained `ImpactDelayTicks` (default 0). Traditional = `TelegraphTicks` ~0, `ImpactDelayTicks` 0 (already worked, no change needed - the state machine always takes a minimum of one tick between "decides to attack" and "resolves" regardless of either value, which is what makes Traditional's "no time to react" feel correct). Jad-style = `TelegraphTicks` > 0, `ImpactDelayTicks` 0 (unchanged default shape; Jad's own three attacks in `JadEncounterSetup.cs` didn't need touching). Doom-style = `ImpactDelayTicks` > 0 (new). All three keep the exact same "`PlayerReactionState` is read only on the literal resolve tick" guarantee - `PrayerCheckTimingTests.cs` covers all three archetypes end-to-end. A future real Doom of Mokhaiotl encounter (Stage 8+) should set its ground-slam attacks' `ImpactDelayTicks` rather than inventing a new mechanism. **Note:** the FIRST implementation of this put the Doom-style delay in a `CombatState.Traveling` phase on `NpcCombatState` itself (the attacker's state machine) - see the ownership-fix entry below for why that was wrong and what replaced it.

**Implemented the damage buffer/queue (2026-10-04), informed by the Phoenix necklace research above:** new `Combat/DamageBuffer.cs` (Core, tested, 6 tests in `DamageBufferTests.cs`) — a per-defender queue of `(amount, ticksRemaining)` entries. `Enqueue(amount, travelTicks)` fixes the amount immediately (nothing later changes it, matching the real mechanic); `Advance(Health)` applies anything whose travel time has elapsed; `Clear()` wipes the whole queue unconditionally — this is the literal Phoenix necklace mechanic, built now even though no player equipment exists yet in this project, specifically so a future defensive item's implementation is "call `Clear()`", not a buffer redesign. New `AttackDefinition.ProjectileTravelTicks` (default 0) is orthogonal to `ImpactDelayTicks`: the latter defers the ROLL itself (Doom-style), this defers only the HP APPLICATION of an already-completed roll (Traditional/Jad-style ranged&magic). `DamageResolver.Roll` (new) rolls without touching Health; every attacker in the project (`PlayerMover`, `NpcCombatant`, `BossCombatant`) now goes through `ICombatTarget.DamageBuffer.Enqueue` instead of applying directly — even melee's 0-travel-tick hits, which still land same-tick because `TickScheduler`'s phase order always runs decision phases (PlayerQueue=0, NpcAi=1) before movement phases (NpcMovement=3, PlayerMovement=4) within one `RunTick()` call, and `Advance` is called from each component's movement phase. Jad's ranged/magic attacks now set `projectileTravelTicks: 1` (serialized field `rangedMagicProjectileTravelTicks` on `JadEncounterSetup`) to demonstrate this live; melee stays 0 correctly. Known accepted trade-off: an HP-threshold trigger (`BossCombatant`'s `TriggerBinding`s) evaluated in `RunBossAi` (phase 1) lags one tick behind a player hit that lands via `DamageBuffer.Advance` in `RunBossMovement` (phase 3) of the SAME tick, because triggers are checked before the buffer advances that tick - not something the user asked to fix, documented in `OSRS_COMBAT_PLAN.md` and in a code comment in case Stage 4's trigger timing ever needs to be exact.

**Ownership fix, user-caught (2026-10-04): "the traveling phase of an attack's lifetime should not belong to the NPC that triggered it."** The Doom-style delay was first built as `NpcCombatState.Traveling` - a state owned by the ATTACKER. That's backwards for the same reason `DamageBuffer` already lives on the defender: once a telegraph ends and an attack launches, its outcome must not depend on the attacker still existing or still being ticked. Concretely, `BossCombatant.RunBossAi`/`NpcCombatant.RunNpcAi` both early-return the instant `actor.Health.IsDead`, so under the old design, killing the attacker mid-travel would silently delete an already-launched, already-telegraphed shot instead of letting it land. Fix: `NpcCombatState` is back to a plain 2-state machine (`Idle`/`Telegraphing`) and ALWAYS resolves the instant a telegraph ends, regardless of `ImpactDelayTicks` - the caller (not this class) decides what to do with that. New `Combat/PendingAttackQueue.cs`, owned by the DEFENDER (added to `ICombatTarget` alongside `DamageBuffer`): when `CurrentAttack.ImpactDelayTicks > 0`, the attacker calls `target.PendingAttacks.Enqueue(attack, attackerTileAtLaunch, impactDelayTicks)` and is immediately free to act again; the queue itself rolls (reading the DEFENDER's live `PlayerReactionState`, passed in by whoever calls `Advance` - null for any non-player target) once the delay elapses, then feeds the result into that same defender's `DamageBuffer`, so it composes correctly with `ProjectileTravelTicks` too. The attacker's tile is captured AT LAUNCH, not re-read later, matching real OSRS tile-anchored hazards (fixed to where cast, not wherever the caster wanders). All three attacker call sites and the Doom-style tests were updated; a shared `FakeCombatTarget.cs` test double was added since testing `PendingAttackQueue` needs a real `ICombatTarget`. **General lesson for future encounter mechanics:** when adding any state that represents "something already launched/committed, awaiting a later resolution," ask whether it should survive the LAUNCHER dying before that resolution - if yes (nearly always, for anything projectile/hazard-shaped), it belongs on the target or on the encounter, never on the attacker's own per-NPC state.

**Follow-up, same day: the user then said the OLD bug's specific outcome - a killed attacker's in-flight shot never landing - was intentional and should be preserved, even though the mechanism producing it (the attacker's own stalled AI loop) was not.** So the fix above was refined rather than reverted: `PendingAttackQueue.Enqueue` now also takes the attacker's live `Health` (captured by reference, not copied), and `Advance` explicitly checks `AttackerHealth.IsDead` at the resolve tick, dropping the entry (no roll, nothing reaches `DamageBuffer`) if true. This converts an accidental side effect into a deliberate, correct check: the desired OUTCOME (dead attacker's unresolved shots vanish) is kept, but it no longer depends on *why* the attacker stopped ticking - previously ANY reason the attacker's component stopped calling `Advance` (disabled, pooled, a future refactor) would have silently orphaned the shot too, not just death specifically. **The general lesson refines to:** separate "should this state's resolution be coupled to the launcher's continued existence" (usually no) from "should this state's resolution be coupled to the launcher's continued ALIVENESS" (ask the user / check the design - sometimes yes, and if so, check it explicitly by reference rather than relying on component lifecycle as a side-channel for it).
